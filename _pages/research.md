## Job Market Paper

<div class="research-card" markdown="1">

### **Get Fit with MA? Estimating the Impact of Medicare Advantage on Physical Activity**

**with Jing Li**

[Paper](https://jason-jongmin-jung.com/files/Jung_Li_2026.pdf)

This paper studies whether Medicare Advantage enrollment increases physical activity around the age-65 Medicare eligibility threshold. Using nationally representative survey data and a regression discontinuity difference-in-differences design, we find a larger increase in daily exercise among MA enrollees relative to the comparison group, with stronger responses among women, non-White adults, and adults without a college degree.

**Topics:** Medicare Advantage · Physical Activity · Healthy Aging · Causal Inference

### **Move to Move? Neighborhood Fitness Culture and Exercise Among Older Adults**

This paper studies whether neighborhood fitness culture affects physical activity among older adults. Using the Health and Retirement Study linked to restricted ZIP-code identifiers, I examine whether changes in neighborhood fitness culture following residential moves are associated with changes in exercise behavior. The analysis combines a mover-based design with event-study evidence to study the role of local environments in shaping preventive health behavior.

**Topics:** Neighborhood Effects · Physical Activity · Healthy Aging

*Available upon request.*

### **Do Unionized Firms Have Better Management Practices?**

**with Arnab Basu**

[Paper](https://jason-jongmin-jung.com/files/Jung_Basu_2026.pdf)

Using restricted data from the World Management Survey, we examine the relationship between labor unions and management practices across private manufacturing firms in Latin America, North America, and Europe. We find that unionized firms are better managed overall, while the relationship differs across management domains. Unionized firms score lower on people-management practices but higher on operations, monitoring, and target-setting practices.

### **Occupational Characteristics and Rural–Urban Disparities in Later-Life Cognitive Performance**

**with Jing Li and William H. Dow**

This project examines whether occupational characteristics help explain rural–urban disparities in later-life cognitive performance. Using Health and Retirement Study data linked to O*NET, the analysis characterizes occupations along cognitive, social-interaction, and machine-related dimensions and studies their contribution to geographic differences in cognitive outcomes.

### **Where Seniors Sweat: Geographic Variation in SilverSneakers Uptake**

**with Liza Rovniak and Dori Rosenberg**

This project studies geographic variation in participation in SilverSneakers among older adults, with a focus on differences in uptake, participation intensity, adherence, and dropout across communities. The analysis examines how local program availability, Medicare Advantage penetration, facility access, and population characteristics are associated with participation in supplemental fitness benefits.
